================================================================

SETUP
-----
Command:   python experiments/run_suite.py   (seeds 0 and 1)
Runs:      logs_active/dqn/SpaceInvadersNoFrameskip-v4_1 .. _10
           _1-_5 = seed 0, _6-_10 = seed 1, config order:
           baseline, lr_high, lr_low, buffer_small, batch_large
Shared:    n_envs 4, train_freq 4, gradient_steps 4 (replay ratio 8),
           n_timesteps 1M (= 4M frames), learning_starts 20k,
           buffer 100k, batch 32, lr 1e-4, target_update 1000,
           eval 10 episodes every 50k steps (non-deterministic: Zoo
           evaluates Atari with the current exploration rate, 0.01

           lr_low       learning_rate 1e-5
           buffer_small buffer_size 10k

FINDINGS
--------
1. The budget matters most. An earlier suite (300k steps, gradient_steps 1,
   ~18k updates per run) left every config at ~250, so they couldn't be told
   apart. With ~250k updates per run, the configs separate clearly and both
   seeds agree.

2. The baseline (standard Atari DQN settings) is best: training reward tripled,
   and it was still rising at 1M steps.

3. lr_high (1e-3) is unstable. Training reward stayed flat at ~200, eval
   repeatedly collapsed to 2-10 points (the policy gets stuck not shooting,
   and 1% random actions aren't enough to break it out), and
   it had the highest loss spikes.

4. lr_low (1e-5) is too slow. It learns, but about 10x more slowly, and ends
   near where baseline was at 200-300k steps.

5. batch_large (128) starts fastest (it led for the first ~40% of the run), then
   flattens at ~450 while baseline keeps climbing. It had the smoothest loss and
   took ~25% longer to run. Likely cause (not verified): each transition is
   sampled ~32x instead of 8x, so the network may overfit the buffer.

6. buffer_small (10k) improves steadily but ends lower. The buffer holds only
   ~3 episodes, so minibatches have little variety.

7. Eval rises and falls even when training goes well (baseline seed 1 peaked at
   694 at ~700k, then fell to ~500). This is normal for DQN, and it's why
   best_model.zip is worth keeping.

8. A score of ~600-900 at 1M steps is in line with typical DQN progress on
   Space Invaders. Fully trained DQN reaches ~1000-2000 after 10M+ steps.


CAVEATS
-------
- Only 2 seeds. The large gaps (baseline vs either lr variant) are reliable.
  The small gap between batch_large and buffer_small is too close to call.
- 1M steps is still early for Atari. Rankings may change in longer runs,
  especially for batch_large.

- Bigger batches start faster but end lower: 32 > 64 > 128 at 1M steps,
  and 128 > 64 > 32 early in the run.
- Batch 64 led baseline through the middle of the run, then baseline passed
  it in the final stretch.
- Unlike batch 128, batch 64 had not stalled: eval kept rising to the end
  (new best at 1M), and its final evals nearly match baseline's. A longer
  run might close the gap.
- Batch 32 is still the best choice for 1M steps; batch 64 is close but
  slower in wall time.
- Caveats: one seed only. Batch 64 with gradient_steps 4 also reuses each
  transition 2x as often as baseline (replay ratio 16), so this does not
  separate batch size from data reuse.
